# Historical dynamic replay

The replay is a research prototype. `sources.py` contains the replaceable measurement source and historical weather prediction adapter; `monitoring.py` assesses the readings available at a decision time; `optimizer.py` reschedules pending jobs; `service.py` orchestrates replay and snapshots. The browser plays back precomputed frames. This is not a live service and does not control appliances.

The original schedule is built by `../milp/optimizer.py`, using `ApplianceJob` and `solve_solar_only`. The application enables its partial-plan option: priority levels are optimized lexicographically, then preferred-start shift and peak power. Unselected runs are reported for that day; they are not moved to another day. The separate `optimizer.py` in this folder revises that plan: `candidate_starts` finds the permitted remaining starts, `fixed_load_at_slot` reserves power for already-started jobs, and `revise` builds and solves the rescheduling MILP. The replay can omit pending runs if not all can fit, favoring higher priority; already-started runs remain fixed. Initially omitted or subsequently removed runs are reported and are not automatically reconsidered later in that replay. `validate_revision` independently checks returned starts and power constraints. `plans.py` translates start indexes into schedule records and simulated slot loads. The rescheduler expects validated 15-minute, single-day inputs and treats a job starting at the decision slot as not yet started. Its `None` result includes infeasible and solver-not-optimal outcomes, so a production caller must not assume it means only infeasibility.

Solar Scheduler builds 14 separate daily plans. Dynamic Replay can take the selected day from a saved matching 14-day plan, including its forecast slots, after validating it against appliance, solar, and household constraints. The frontend requires the saved historical plan to match the selected date, Meepe coordinates, PV capacity, five-minute model and appliance/settings snapshot. Otherwise it clearly says the replay will generate a separate initial plan. This historical replay still covers one day, and does not monitor or reschedule a live 14-day plan.

On the frontend, `dynamic-replay/supply-estimates.ts` contains the reusable simulated supply calculation; `components/supply-summary.tsx` only displays it. Comments near the calculations explain the slot conversion and missing-data assumptions.

At each 15-minute boundary a user may choose the latest reading (no more than 10 minutes old), or an unweighted mean of readings from the preceding 30 or 60 minutes. For the average method, each reading is paired with the latest prediction at or before that reading. At least four observations for 30 minutes or seven for 60 minutes are required, the first and last must be within 10 minutes of the window edges, and gaps cannot exceed 10 minutes. The sample average is not a time-weighted integral. A trigger requires both the absolute and relative thresholds, a reference prediction of at least 0.2 kW, and a one-hour cooldown since the last trigger. Thresholds and the cooldown are experimental.

On a trigger the ratio of observed to predicted power (bounded between zero and one) scales the original solar predictions for the next hour. The optimizer keeps started jobs fixed and searches for pending-job starts. It retains the old plan if no feasible optimum is proven. Each trigger records the comparison values, before and proposed/after schedules, and outcome. Running states are simulated from schedules, not read from devices. A running load may already exceed observed power; rescheduling cannot undo that shortage.

The historical weather source is reanalysis, reconstructed after the event. Although the trigger never accesses future inverter observations, the reconstructed weather used to create the baseline can itself contain hindsight. The replay therefore is not a leak-free day-ahead evaluation. A live system requires predictions issued before the decision, real-time sensor and appliance state, a durable event store and explicit control and acknowledgment. For research background on causal forecast evaluation see https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73817-3 and the persistence discussion at https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00521-026-12335-1.

The display estimates accumulated energy from completed 15-minute point samples, with missing-sample coverage shown. It is not measured interval energy or a complete household meter reading. Confirm `pac` units, site capacity, time alignment, data quality and device ownership before publication.

The supply card uses the simulated planned load plus configured background load. For each covered slot it estimates solar used as min(load, measured PV) and grid needed as max(load − measured PV, 0), multiplied by 0.25 hours. It assumes an available grid and no battery; it does not change the solar-only optimizer. These are illustrative estimates, not measured imports or whole-house consumption. Unknown slots are excluded.
